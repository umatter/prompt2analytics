## Model and estimator

Standard-form VARMA with every coefficient free:

```
y_t = c + A_1 y_{t-1} + ... + A_p y_{t-p} + u_t + M_1 u_{t-1} + ... + M_q u_{t-q}.
```

Hannan & Rissanen (1982) two-step estimator:

1. **Step 1 (q > 0).** Fit a long VAR(m) with intercept by OLS on t = m + 1..n. Its residuals ê_t
   estimate u_t. Only the residuals are needed, so the diagonally equilibrated normal equations are
   solved by Cholesky; when the factorisation fails or the ratio of its largest to its smallest
   diagonal entry exceeds 1e4 (a heuristic gate: the squared ratio is a lower bound on the condition
   number of the equilibrated matrix, not the condition number itself), the pivoted QR of
   `stats::lm.fit` is used instead (an aliased lag
   then gets the warning "long VAR (step 1): Coefficients not defined because of singularities: ..").
   The cross products Z'Z and Z'Y are not formed from the lag matrix: the block of lags (a, a + d)
   is the lagged sum `sum y_s y_{s-d}'` over a window of rows, computed by dot products for lag 0
   and moved by one row (one row in, one out) for each further lag, and the residuals are formed
   from the series' columns. That is O(n K^2 m) operations instead of O(n K^2 m^2)
   (`test_varma_lagged_cross_products_match_lag_matrix` holds them to the lag-matrix products at
   1e-12). The step-2 coefficients that follow agree with the R case (`lm.fit` throughout) to about
   1e-14.
2. **Step 2.** For each equation, fit OLS of y_t on `[1, y_{t-1..p}, ê_{t-1..q}]` over
   t = m + max(p, q) + 1..n, by the pivoted QR of `lm.fit`. This gives n_eff = n - m - max(p, q)
   observations. statsmodels' `hannan_rissanen` starts at m + q + 1 when m >= p, so for p > q it
   uses p - q more observations; p2a keeps one rule for every (p, q), so that fits at the same m
   share their sample and their criteria compare.

Reported quantities:
- **Coefficients.** In the order `const`, `<v>.l<j>` (as vars names them) and `e.<v>.l<j>`.
  `ar_params` holds the constant and the AR rows; `ma_params` holds the MA rows.
- **Standard errors, t and p values: q = 0 only.** For q = 0 the fit is an OLS VAR and the
  `summary.lm` standard errors (df = n_eff - rank) are valid. For q > 0 they are **not reported**
  (`std_errors` is empty, `t_values()` and `p_values()` return empty vectors, and Display says so):
  the step-2 regressors ê_{t-j} are generated by step 1, and for the AR and constant coefficients the
  step-1 estimation error enters the limiting distribution whenever M != 0 (a generated-regressor
  problem), so the step-2 OLS covariance does not describe it. A Monte Carlo on the case DGP (500
  replications, m = floor(ln(n)^2) = 35) put the ratio of the mean OLS SE to the empirical SD of
  the estimate at 0.86-1.07, lowest (0.86-0.90) for the constant and AR terms of equation y1, whose
  MA row is non-zero.
- **Sigma_u.** U'U / n_eff (ML).
- **Log-likelihood.** logLik = -n_eff/2 (K ln 2 pi + ln det Sigma_u + K), with ln det Sigma_u from the
  Cholesky factor (the old code used the product of the absolute values of all K^2 entries).
- **Information criteria.** AIC = -2 logLik + 2 npar and BIC = -2 logLik + ln(n_eff) npar, where
  npar = K x rank of the step-2 design. This is the parameter count of `stats::logLik` for
  `vars::VAR`, extended by the MA coefficients.
- An aliased step-2 regressor is NaN (R: NA) with R's warning "Coefficients not defined because of
  singularities: ..", and its name is in `aliased`; SEs (q = 0) and npar use the identified columns.
  `run_var` refuses the same design instead (`SingularMatrix`).

**Long-VAR order m.**

| Situation | m |
|---|---|
| `long_var_order` given | that value. It must be >= 1 when q > 0, and the long VAR must keep a residual degree of freedom. With q = 0 it only moves the sample start, so the criteria compare with VARMA(p, q) fits at the same m. |
| default, q = 0 | 0. The fit is the OLS VAR(p), equal to `vars::VAR(type = "const")`. |
| default, q > 0 | max(floor(2 ln n), 2 max(p, q)): 9 at n = 100, 11 at n = 400, 13 at n = 1,000, 18 at n = 10,000. It is reduced to floor((n - 2) / (2K + 1)) with a warning, so that the long VAR keeps at least as many residual degrees of freedom as regressors. Too short when an MA root is near the unit circle (see below): set `long_var_order` larger there. |

**Why 2 ln n.** Hannan & Rissanen need a long-VAR order that grows with n; Hannan &
Kavalieris (1984) justify orders growing like ln n, and AIC itself picks an order of that rate.
That justification needs the MA roots bounded away from the unit circle: the truncation bias of a
long VAR(m) decays like rho^m, with rho the largest modulus of an inverse MA root (an eigenvalue of
M_1 for q = 1), and m = c ln n makes it n^(c ln rho), below the n^(-1/2) of the sampling error
only when rho < exp(-1/(2c)). For c = 2 that is rho below about 0.78. With an MA root nearer the
unit circle the default long VAR is too short and `long_var_order` should be set larger, for
example to the earlier default floor(ln(n)^2).

The earlier default was floor(ln(n)^2) (Gómez & Maravall 2001, statsmodels' `hannan_rissanen`
default): 47 at n = 1,000 and 84 at n = 10,000, which made the long VAR 28-78 times the cost of
the old (incorrect) estimator, which fitted no long VAR. A proposed max(p + q, floor(1.5 ln(n)^2))
was rejected by an earlier Monte Carlo of the case DGP (coefficient RMSE 1.35 at n = 100 against
0.77 for floor(ln(n)^2), at 2.25 times the cost). The order chosen by AIC
(`vars::VARselect(lag.max = 10, type = "const")`, the convention of R's VAR packages) was the
third candidate. The Monte Carlo
[`validation/r/mc/varma_long_var_order.R`](../../r/mc/varma_long_var_order.R) (`Rscript`, about
20 s; not an R reference case) compares the three with the case's estimator, 200 replications per
cell and seed 20261104, on three VARMA(1,1) designs: the case DGP (inverse MA roots 0.3 and
-0.25), "strong MA" (the case's A_1 and Sigma, inverse MA roots 0.8 and -0.7) and a trivariate
design (inverse MA roots of modulus 0.42 and less). Every order is reduced to
floor((n - 2) / (2K + 1)) as the default is. Entries are the root mean squared error of the
K (1 + 2K) step-2 coefficients (all K equations together):

| DGP | n | floor(ln(n)^2) | floor(2 ln n) | AIC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| case | 100 | 0.716 | 0.800 | 2.719 |
| case | 400 | 0.507 | 0.527 | 0.627 |
| case | 2,000 | 0.330 | 0.275 | 0.294 |
| strong MA | 100 | 1.054 | 0.897 | 0.859 |
| strong MA | 400 | 0.725 | 0.455 | 0.417 |
| strong MA | 2,000 | 0.400 | 0.225 | 0.208 |
| K = 3 | 100 | 0.911 | 0.909 | 8.071 |
| K = 3 | 400 | 0.562 | 0.599 | 0.762 |
| K = 3 | 2,000 | 0.384 | 0.332 | 0.339 |

floor(2 ln n) loses to floor(ln(n)^2) on the case DGP at small n (12% at n = 100, 4% at
n = 400) and on the trivariate design at n = 400 (7%), and wins in the other cells, by up to 44%
(the long VAR of floor(ln(n)^2) adds estimation noise faster than it removes truncation bias at
these sample sizes, even for the strong MA's root of 0.8). AIC is within 10% of floor(2 ln n) in
five of the nine cells, slightly better with a strong MA part, but erratic in small samples: a few
replications with very large errors put its RMSE at 2.7 on the case DGP and 8.1 on the trivariate
design at n = 100. A data-independent order also keeps the default reproducible from n alone.

**Errors.**

| Condition | Error |
|---|---|
| p = q = 0 | `InvalidSpecification` |
| `long_var_order = 0` with q > 0 | `InvalidSpecification` |
| Too few observations for the long VAR (required = m + 1 + K m + 1) or for the step-2 regression (required = m + max(p, q) + 1 + K (p + q) + K: the K x K Sigma_u needs K residual degrees of freedom); no default long VAR fits (n < 2K + 3) | `InsufficientData` |
| Linearly dependent series (Sigma_u of rank < K) | `SingularMatrix` |
| Missing or non-numeric values | `NullValues` / `NonNumericColumn` (Fail policy, as `vars::VAR`) |

**Not implemented:**
- the third (bias-correction) step;
- ML refinement;
- echelon-form identification restrictions. An over-parameterised VARMA with common factors is
  estimated but not detected, and its MA/AR split can be weakly identified.

**Cost.** One long VAR(m) by its lagged cross products, O(n K^2 m + (K m)^3) with m about 2 ln n,
where the old code fitted one VAR(p) (and no long VAR at all).

**Timing.** Default VARMA(1, 1) against `ff8e7559` (which fitted a VAR(1) and no long VAR):
release build, one core (`taskset`, `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1`), three interleaved rounds; new / base as
the ratio of medians.

| K | n = 100 (m = 9) | n = 1,000 (m = 13) | n = 10,000 (m = 18) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 2.93 | 2.24 | 2.08 |
| 3 | 2.77 | 2.30 | 2.80 |

The 2.1-2.9x is within the factor of about 4.5 the maintainer accepted for fitting the long VAR,
which the base did not do. At the earlier order floor(ln(n)^2), with the lag-matrix Gram, the fit
cost 5.4-77.5 times the base. At a fixed m = 7 and K = 3 the lagged cross products cut the time
from 72 to 58 us at n = 100 and from 10.0 to 3.9 ms at n = 10,000 (measured on a loaded machine),
with the same AIC to 10 digits.

## R reference

No R package for this estimator is installed (MTS, marima and dse are missing). The estimator is
written out with `lm.fit`/`lm` in `validation/r/cases/ts_varma.R`, case `varma_hr`. The data is a
bivariate VARMA(1,1) with n = 400 and seed 20261114
(`validation/datasets/generated/varma_sim.csv`). For q = 0 the reference is
`vars::VAR(p = 2, type = "const")`. R 4.3.3, vars 1.6.1. The case records standard errors and
p-values for the q = 0 fits only.
